# Setting up GitHub (for complete beginners)

Never used GitHub? Start here. By the end you'll have an account, a secure login, and a profile that's ready to contribute. Takes about 15 minutes. Do this **before** the workshop.

> **What is GitHub?** A website where people store code and work on it together. Think of it as Google Drive for code, but with a full history of every change and a way for many people to contribute to the same project safely. Your GitHub profile is also your public portfolio — recruiters look at it.

---

## 1. Create your account

1. Go to [github.com](https://github.com) and click **Sign up**.
2. Enter your email. **Use an email you'll keep** — your student email is fine, but a personal Gmail is safer long-term (you lose access to student email after you graduate).
3. Create a password. Make it strong; this account will eventually hold your work.
4. **Pick your username carefully.** This is your identity and it shows up in every URL and every contribution. You can't easily change it later.
   - ✅ Good: `waasila-asif`, `wasifdev`, `w-asif`
   - ❌ Avoid: `cutiepie2004`, `xX_coder_Xx`, random numbers
   - A clean username is a professional asset. Treat it like you'd treat an email address on a CV.
5. Solve the puzzle to prove you're human, then click **Create account**.

## 2. Verify your email

1. GitHub sends a code to your email. Enter it.
2. **You must verify before you can do anything**, including contributing to a repo. If the email doesn't arrive, check spam, or click resend.

## 3. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) — required

GitHub **requires 2FA** for anyone who contributes code. Set it up now so it doesn't block you during the workshop.

1. Click your avatar (top right) → **Settings** → **Password and authentication**.
2. Under **Two-factor authentication**, click **Enable 2FA**.
3. Easiest method: an **authenticator app** on your phone (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or Authy). Install one, then scan the QR code GitHub shows.
4. Enter the 6-digit code the app generates.
5. **Save your recovery codes** somewhere safe (not your phone). These get you back in if you lose your phone. Screenshot them and store them somewhere you won't lose — losing both your phone and these codes means losing the account.

## 4. Set up your profile (5 minutes, worth it)

A filled-in profile makes you look real, not like a bot. Settings → **Public profile**:

- **Profile photo** — a clear photo of your face, or a clean avatar. Not the grey default.
- **Name** — your real name.
- **Bio** — one line: `CS student @ NUST | learning systems & security`.
- **Location** and any links (LinkedIn, portfolio) — optional but nice.

## 5. Claim your student perks (optional, do it later)

As a student you get the **GitHub Student Developer Pack** — free access to a pile of developer tools and cloud credits. Apply at [education.github.com/pack](https://education.github.com/pack) with your student email. Not needed for the workshop, but worth grabbing.
